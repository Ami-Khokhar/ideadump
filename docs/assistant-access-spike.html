The question

Can ChatGPT, or any assistant, read a person's TapLog expenses? Four answers survive contact with the platforms. One of them costs a day.

Research only: no production code, and nothing in the decision log yet.

Who may read it

Answer the four questions Read the evidence

The verdict

Ship the export pack. Put the real effort into Siri, not into a server.

A ChatGPT side needs an OpenAI Platform organisation before a single expense is read, and a hosted one is months of work plus the privacy promise we currently sell against. The Apple path needs no account, no endpoint and no change to the policy, and it works with just the phone.

TapLog keeps every record on one phone, with no account and no sync, and gives the file away free, because that is the only exit the data has. Any assistant that reads those records crosses that line, and what crossing it costs depends entirely on which side the reading happens.

What is real. One iPhone, one store, two working intents, one free export. No server, no account, and no telemetry anywhere in the repository, so nothing today measures whether anybody wants this.

What changed. ChatGPT now takes a custom MCP server by URL or by tunnel, and Apple now hands an app the on-device model. Both facts are newer than the issue that asked the question.

What it costs. One working day for the export pack, three for the Apple path, ten for a self-hosted bridge, and a season for anything hosted with accounts.

What I need. Four decisions: which shape goes first, what counts as success, whether the technical bridge belongs in the record, and who writes the prompts.

01 — Evidence

What the platforms allow today

Read from the vendor documentation on 9 November 2026. The issue was filed on an assumption that no longer holds.
FactConsequence hereSource
ChatGPT and Codex share one Plugins directory. A custom MCP server is added by URL, with OAuth, no auth, or both. The connector path is supported and documented, not a hack. plugins/quickstart
Secure MCP Tunnel links a private stdio or HTTP server on the person's own machine to ChatGPT with no inbound port. It needs a Platform organisation, a tunnel id and a runtime API key, and cannot be distributed publicly. The self-hosted bridge is cheaper than the issue assumed, and still out of reach for someone who only owns a phone. secure-mcp-tunnels
Public distribution of an MCP-backed plugin needs a stable public HTTPS endpoint, a verified identity, a ZIP and a review. A hosted connector is a product and an operations job, not a feature. plugins/deploy/submission
Local stdio plugins exist for the desktop, but local submissions are limited to approved partners, allowlisted by OpenAI. We cannot publish "TapLog reads your phone through your Mac" to anyone. plugins/deploy/desktop-only
Adding a custom MCP server happens on the web, and stays subject to workspace policy and Lockdown. The setup moment is a laptop and an administrator, not the phone in a shop. custom-mcp-server
Apple's App Intents make an app's content and actions discoverable by Apple Intelligence, and drive Siri, Spotlight, Shortcuts and widgets. Foundation Models covers on-device and Private Cloud Compute models with tool calling. The same question is answerable with no account and no server, on the phone. developer.apple.com

Read as documentation, not exercised. I did not add a custom MCP server, run a tunnel client, or submit a plugin, and the Apple pages were read rather than tested against this app's iOS 17 deployment target.

The CSV exporter already owns quoting, the archived and pending rules, and the temporary file lifecycle, so this is a second formatter over content that is already correct. It stays free: export is not a convenience on top of the product, it is the only exit the data has.

Because it is a file the person shares, nothing leaves the phone until they choose, and the app sends nothing to anyone. The privacy policy does not change by a word.

What a hosted shape would need

  • An account, a sync service and an auth server, each with its own failure mode.
  • An OpenAI review, and an App Store consent screen naming a third-party AI.
  • A retention policy, a processor list, and a breach plan.

What would reopen it

  • People asking for assistant access in numbers the export pack cannot hold.
  • A second device, which forces sync anyway, and sync makes a server honest.
  • Revenue that pays for the operations, rather than a hope that it might.

06 — Yours

Four things to settle

Each answer changes what I build next. Pin a comment on the one you disagree with, anywhere on this page.

A or D first? My order is A, then D, because A is a day's work and D changes what Siri offers. If you would rather the Apple path came first, say so and I will re-plan the two.

Is one in a hundred fair? That is the share of recap opens that would have to become assistant shares for the export to earn its place after two weeks. Too strict, and it dies on silence. Too loose, and it survives on nothing.

Does the self-hosted bridge belong in the record? It is real and documented, and it needs an OpenAI Platform organisation, so it is for the person who already has one. I would write it down as "possible, not distributed" rather than leave it out.

Who writes the prompts? I can draft all six against a real export from the simulator. They read better if you use them first, then tell me which ones you actually reached for.
