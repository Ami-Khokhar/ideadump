TapLog — marketing, paywall & revenue plan

Built from the shipped code (ProGate, PaywallView, PaywallEvidence), the paywall brief, the indie payment-patterns research, the existing distribution kit, and the legal report's pricing facts. The pricing model is treated as fixed: one non-consumable, no subscription — the code already ships that way and the reasoning below stays inside it.

The one-sentence plan: market the 5-second capture and the privacy, let the free grove do the selling, and ask for money only at the two moments the user reaches for more — because with a one-time purchase, the paywall has exactly one chance per user, and the trigger moment carries all the weight.

1What actually sells this app

Not a feature list. Two arguments, in this order:

1 · "It takes five seconds, from anywhere"

Demonstrable in one video: Action Button press → keypad → amount → Log → haptic → pocket. No finance app in the top charts can shoot that ad. The hook is capture friction, and the demo IS the marketing.

2 · "Your spending never leaves your phone"

Verifiable: no account, no bank login, no server, no analytics, no tracking — and CSV export free. This is a structural position incumbents can't copy (their business model is the data flow). Post-Mint, it's a purchase driver.

The grove is the third argument and the one that closes: it's the screenshot people share, the reason the app is memorable, and — since the current paywall line — the thing Pro unlocks. Earlier marketing drafts sold a different app than the one built; the grove now is the product's face and belongs in every asset.

2The paywall: the line, and when it appears

2.1 · The line (as shipped — endorsed)

Free, foreverPro — one payment
All capture from all 7 front doorsA budget tree for every category
Unlimited entries & history, categoriesThe monthly recap (weekly stays free)
3 budget trees
Weekly recap · streaks · CSV export (free by principle)

Why this line is right: capture is the habit, and the habit must form before anything can be sold — gating it would kill the product before the pitch. Three trees is enough to feel the mechanic (one tree is a progress bar; three start reading as a grove) while the argument for Pro is visual: something thriving beside something wilting. The weekly recap — the thing the one notification is about, the retention loop's backbone — stays free so the loop never breaks for non-payers. CSV stays free because it's the only exit the data has; charging for it converts the privacy promise into a hostage situation (and an angry review).

The fairness rule that's already coded: deleting a budget returns the free slot, and existing budgets are never taken away. Gate only addition, never keeping.

2.2 · When the paywall appears (and when it never does)

Trigger map
Both triggers are intent moments: the user has just tried to do the thing Pro does. By then the paywall can say something no competitor's can — PaywallEvidence reads the user's real record ("143 expenses · 24 days · ₹18,400 tracked") and shows it only once there are ≥8 entries over ≥3 days; below that, thin numbers would argue against paying, so the grove illustration shows instead. The sentence that sells is the one the user writes in their own head while reading their own numbers.
Natural trigger timing (what to expect): trigger 1 (4th tree) fires early for eager users — sometimes week 1 — and is the higher-volume moment. Trigger 2 (monthly recap) fires around the first month boundary and is the higher-conviction moment: small-money patterns need a month to become visible, so the month view is where the insight actually lives. Two triggers at different depths of engagement is exactly what a one-shot paywall wants. Do not add a third entry point, a settings nudge, or a "Pro" badge anywhere in the capture path.

2.3 · Paywall mechanics (already correct in code — keep them)

  • Price from displayPrice (never hard-coded), fetched only when the paywall shows.
  • "Not now" dismisses; unlock dismisses instantly; cancellation is silence — never an argument.
  • Restore always reachable (paywall + Settings); empty restore says so plainly.
  • Copy fix before launch (from the checklist): "One payment, yours for good" → "One-time purchase. No subscription. Restore anytime." — an ownership claim is a legal problem, not just a style choice.
  • No timers, no countdowns, no confirm-shaming, no repeat pop-ups — those are the dark patterns India's CCPA guidelines and the UK DMCC Act now target, and they'd poison the trust the app's entire pitch rests on.

EU$4.99 equivalent, storefronts deselected at launchTurned on after DSA trader verification clears (see checklist §2). A few weeks of EU revenue is the cost of keeping a home address off public pages.
  • Small Business Program (apply day 1): commission 30% → 15%. Net per sale ≈ $4.24 (before taxes) instead of $3.49. At this price point the program literally doubles the margin.
  • No launch discount — one price, one product, permanently. Changing a listed price later is allowed (and a $4.99→$7.99 move with a v2 feature is a real lever); "launch special" urgency mechanics aren't worth the copy risk.
  • Offer codes, not discounts: Apple's offer-code system (up to 25,000/quarter for IAPs) is the clean way to seed communities and creators — a free unlock costs nothing and buys goodwill, a discount just reprices the product.
  • Never a family-share question mark: consider enabling Family Sharing on the non-consumable at setup — it's free generosity that reads as confidence, and reviewers like it.

The honest read: at $4.99 one-time, this is a validation business, not an income business. Even the good case is a few hundred dollars — and that's fine, because it's the number that de-risks the decision. The Day-30/90 gates below exist to answer one question: does a 40%+ D30 logging habit exist, and do 3–5% of engaged users pay? If yes, that's the asset — it justifies v2 investment (iCloud sync and multi-device as a second Pro tier or a paid major version, the two highest-value additions this architecture lacks). If no, the app remains a portfolio piece, and nothing was spent finding out.

Revenue levers, in order of leverage

  1. Distribution, not price. The patterns research is blunt: the build is weeks; distribution is the moat. Every hour goes to the channels below before any pricing debate.
  2. EU storefronts on (post-verification) — instant market expansion, zero work.
  3. Grove shareability. The grove is the artifact people screenshot. A tasteful "share my grove" image export (health states, no amounts) in v1.1 turns users into the acquisition channel — the free tier doing the marketing, deliberately.
  4. Localization (Hindi first) once Indian traction shows; the ASO keywords then move into a proper India localization.
  5. Price ladder via v2 value, never via a second tier: sync/multi-device justifies $9.99 for new buyers while grandfathering everyone (fairness rule, already the app's ethos).

Day-90 questions (the real decision)

  • Is D30 logging retention ≥40%? (the metric that proves capture-first)
  • What share of paywall impressions converted? <2% → the trigger moments or the offer, not the price, need work. >5% → consider the v2 price ladder.
  • Did any single channel produce >50% of installs? Double down on it; drop the rest.

7Risks & anti-patterns (decided, not open)

  • No subscription, ever, for what's local. Subscription-on-purely-local-utility is a documented backlash pattern; the anti-subscription stance is also a marketing asset. If recurring revenue ever matters, it arrives as new server-backed value (sync), priced separately.
  • No ads, no data sales, no analytics. The privacy brand can never violate itself.
  • Category crowding (4.3(b)): Finance is the most crowded shelf on the store. The Review Notes statement (manual-only, no bank connections, no advice) plus the distinctive grove visual identity are the differentiators — keep screenshots visually unmistakable.
  • One-shot paywall anxiety: resist the urge to "improve" conversion with interruption. The line's integrity is worth more than a point of conversion; one angry "paywall ambush" review outlives a hundred installs.
  • Meta-lesson to keep on the wall: users pay for what they can't comfortably operate without. This app's daily-use loop (capture → streak → recap) is the P3/P4 pattern stack — the job is distribution, and the grove is the word-of-mouth engine.
