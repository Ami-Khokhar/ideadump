## A3. App Store Connect name uniqueness, and what happens if another developer holds the name

### Takeaway
App Store names must be unique across the whole store. A name is reserved as soon as any developer creates an app record with it, even before release. The bare name "TapLog" is taken. "TapLog: Expense Tracker" is a different string, so App Store Connect will probably accept it (not confirmed; test it by creating the app record). If a name is blocked, the only formal route is Apple's App Name Dispute form. That route works best if you hold a trademark. Apple then puts the two parties in contact; it does not decide the dispute.

### Cited Findings
- App Review Guideline 2.3.7: "Choose a unique app name…"; app names are limited to 30 characters; no trademarked terms or popular app names used to game search — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Guideline 4.1(c): "You cannot use another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name, without approval from the developer." Guideline 4.1(a) warns against "minor changes to another app's name" — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Guideline 5.2.1: do not use third-party trademarks without permission, and no "copycat" names or metadata — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- App Store Connect: the name must be 2–30 characters, and you can edit it until you submit to App Review — [App Store Connect Help – App information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-information)
- Secondary: a name is "in use" as soon as an app record is created. It stays reserved while the app is in development, and App Store Connect blocks a name that another developer has taken — [PTKD Journal](https://ptkd.com/journal/app-store-same-name-availability); [Apple Developer Forums thread 706597](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/706597)
- Apple's App Name Dispute form covers apps that block use of a trademark as an app name. Apple identifies the blocking app(s) and "in most cases" asks that provider to work with the claimant directly — [Apple Legal – App Store Dispute Forms](https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/dispute-forms/app-store/)
- Forum reports: disputes took from about 2 days to a few weeks. Developers who hold a trademark are told to file a name dispute — [Apple Developer Forums 122990](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/122990); [Apple Developer Forums 5464](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/5464)

### Inferences
- "TapLog: Expense Tracker" is 23 characters, which is within the 30-character limit. The existing "TapLog" is a different product name, so 4.1(c) and 5.2.1 are a reviewer-discretion risk rather than a certain rejection. If Peter Underwood files a complaint, TapLog has no trademark to show, and he has the earlier use.
- Fallback: use a distinctive prefix or a changed name (for example "TapLog Budget", or a new coined word) if App Store Connect refuses the name, or if Apple forwards a content dispute.

### Gaps
- I found no official Apple page that states the exact matching rule (whether the "TapLog:" prefix counts as a conflict with "TapLog"). Verify by creating the app record in App Store Connect. That also reserves the name.

## A4. Filing a trademark in India (TM-A) and via Madrid: cost, timeline, process, and whether to file before launch

### Takeaway
The India e-filing fee is ₹4,500 per class for an individual applicant (₹9,000 for companies). An individual needs no DPIIT or Udyam certificate to get the lower rate. Filing on a "proposed to be used" basis is allowed, and ™ can be used right after filing. Registration takes about 12–18 months if nobody objects. Expedited examination costs ₹20,000 per class for individuals. Madrid needs an Indian base application first, costs a WIPO basic fee of CHF 653 plus designation fees per country, and needs an IP India handling fee (secondary sources only). Filing before launch is not legally required, but filing early fixes your priority date.

### Cited Findings
- Secondary: TM-A fee is ₹4,500 per class (e-filing) or ₹5,000 (physical) for individuals, startups and small enterprises; ₹9,000 (e-filing) or ₹10,000 (physical) for others; source is the First Schedule of the Trade Marks Rules, 2017 — [Intepat (7 Jan 2026)](https://www.intepat.com/blog/trademark-registration-fees-india); same numbers at [RegisterKaro](https://www.registerkaro.in/post/trademark-registration-fees-in-india-a-complete-guide)
- Secondary: expedited examination is ₹20,000 per class for individuals/MSMEs (e-filing) and ₹40,000 for companies — [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/trademark-registration-fees-india)
- Secondary: startups must upload a DPIIT certificate and small enterprises an Udyam certificate to claim the concession. The applicant type is chosen on Form TM-A — [Intepat startup IP costs](https://www.intepat.com/blog/startup-ip-costs-india)
- Official fee page (not retrieved; 404 during this session): [IP India – Form and Fees](https://ipindia.gov.in/trademarks/form_and_fees_tm)
- Secondary: registration usually takes 12–18 months if there are no objections. With expedited examination, the first examination report comes in 5–10 working days and the total time is about 6–8 months. You must reply to an objection within 30 days. ™ can be used right after filing; ® only after registration — [Vakilsearch](https://vakilsearch.com/article/trademark-registration-timeline-status-check-india/); [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/how-long-does-trademark-registration-take-in-india/). Rana](https://ssrana.in/ufaqs/cost-filing-international-application-madrid-protocol/)

### Inferences
- Minimum India cost: ₹4,500 for one class (class 9). ₹9,000 for classes 9 and 42. Class 36 (financial information) is optional and could draw scrutiny because it is a financial-services class.
- US designation via Madrid: the USPTO individual fee for each class is several hundred CHF. Use the WIPO calculator for the exact amount; I did not compute it here. Because TapLog is only a candidate brand and is weak (A1/A2), consider filing in India first, then designating the US/EU through Madrid within the 6-month Paris priority window, and only if the app gets traction.
- Filing before launch is not required. It is still cheap insurance: filing before or at the Sept 2026 launch fixes your date ahead of any later filer, and gives you a basis for an Apple name dispute.

## B1. India: does a manual expense tracker need an RBI, SEBI or other licence?

### Takeaway
No Indian financial licence that I could find applies to an app that only records amounts the user types in. The RBI-regulated categories are all triggered by activities TapLog does not do: collecting financial information from financial institutions (Account Aggregators), lending or helping others lend (Digital Lending Directions 2025), and payments. SEBI registration is triggered by investment advice, and SEBI's definition of that includes "financial planning" tied to securities or investment products. SMS reading is controlled by Play-store policy rather than an RBI licence.

### Cited Findings
- Account Aggregator: the business of an AA is "retrieving or collecting financial information pertaining to its customer" and consolidating/presenting it. No company may carry on AA business without an RBI certificate of registration. Only NBFCs with at least ₹2 crore net owned fund qualify — [RBI Master Direction NBFC-AA 2016 (copy)](https://www.agloc.org/pdf/NBFCAcctAggregator%20Directions-02-09-16.PDF); [DFS – Account Aggregator Framework](https://financialservices.gov.in/account-aggregator-framework); [Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/account-aggregators/)
- Digital lending: RBI (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 took effect 8 May 2025. They define a Digital Lending App (DLA) as an app "that facilitate[s] digital lending services" for Regulated Entities or their Lending Service Providers (LSPs); LSPs are agents doing lending functions — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/the-rbis-digital-lending-directions-2025-a-unified-code-for-a-fragmented-sector/); [AZB & Partners PDF](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/AZB-Update-Digital-Lending-Directions-2025.pdf) (secondary)
- SEBI IA Regulations 2013, Reg. 2(1): "investment advice" means advice relating to investing in, purchasing, selling or otherwise dealing in securities or investment products, and advice on an investment portfolio, "and shall include financial planning". "Financial planning" is analysis of the client's current financial situation, identification of goals, and "developing and recommending financial strategies" — [BCAS copy of regulations](https://www.bcasonline.org/Referencer2015-16/Other%20Laws/sebi_investment_advisers_regulations_2013.html); current consolidated text: [SEBI (amended 25 Nov 2025)](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/nov-2025/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-investment-advisers-regulations-2013-and-securities-last-amended-on-november-25-2025-_98246.html)
- SEBI FAQ: advice given through widely available public media is not investment advice; no one may act as an IA without SEBI registration — [SEBI FAQ on IA Regulations](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/1424862077270.pdf)
- SMS reading: on Google Play, READ_SMS/RECEIVE_SMS are limited to default SMS handlers and approved exceptions. "SMS-based money management" is a narrow exception, and budgeting or loan apps "may not exfiltrate or share non-financial or personal SMS history" — [Google Play SMS & Call Log policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820)

### Inferences
- TapLog does not retrieve data from financial institutions, lend, move money, or recommend investments. So none of the AA, DLA/LSP, PA/PPI or SEBI-IA regimes applies. The one line to avoid crossing: features that recommend savings or investment products or "strategies" (for example, "move ₹X into a mutual fund") could come near SEBI's "financial planning" limb. Keep the weekly recap descriptive ("you spent X on Y") rather than prescriptive about investments.
- Data protection (the DPDP Act 2023) is separate from financial regulation. It matters little while all data stays on the device and nothing is sent to the developer. Re-check this if cloud sync or analytics is added.

### Gaps
- No RBI or SEBI statement that says outright "manual budgeting apps need no licence" was found. The conclusion comes from the scope of each regime, and no regulator has confirmed it.
- Payment aggregator/PPI rules and the DPDP Act were not researched in depth, because they are clearly outside TapLog's features.

## B2. US and EU: licence or registration for a budgeting app that touches no bank data

### Takeaway
EU: PSD2 registration applies only to "account information services", which access and consolidate payment-account data held at other payment service providers. A tool where the user types in the numbers is outside that definition. US: the CFPB §1033 rule places obligations on data providers and on third parties that get consumer data from them. In any case, it is enjoined and under reconsideration in 2026. So no US or EU financial licence applies to TapLog as described.

### Cited Findings
- PSD2 (Directive 2015/2366) Art. 4(16) defines an AIS as an online service giving consolidated information on one or more payment accounts the user holds with other payment service provider(s). AIS providers must register (Art. 33) and hold professional indemnity insurance (Recital 35). Recital 28 describes AIS as "aggregated online information on one or more payment accounts" — [EUR-Lex PSD2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32015L2366)
- CFPB final §1033 rule issued 22 Oct 2024. Largest providers were due to comply from 1 April 2026 — [CFPB – Personal Financial Data Rights](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/personal-financial-data-rights/); [SIA Partners](https://www.sia-partners.com/en/insights/publications/cfpa-personal-financial-data-rights-part-1033)
- Status: the E.D. Kentucky court enjoined CFPB enforcement until reconsideration is done. The CFPB published an ANPR on reconsideration on 22 Aug 2025 — [Cozen O'Connor 2026 alert](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2026/section-1033-compliance-date-open-banking-rule-enjoined-and-under-reconsideration) (secondary); [Moore & Van Allen](https://www.mvalaw.com/data-points/cfpb-enjoined-from-enforcing-personal-financial-data-rights-rule-1033) (secondary); [Federal Register ANPR](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/22/2025-16139/personal-financial-data-rights-reconsideration); [CFPB reconsideration page](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/rules-under-development/personal-financial-data-rights-reconsideration/)
- The rule text is at [eCFR 12 CFR Part 1033](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1033). It was not retrieved; the site redirected to a bot check.

### Inferences
- PSD2's AIS registration and §1033's "authorized third party" duties both begin only when an app pulls data from a bank or card issuer. TapLog does not, so neither applies. If a future version adds bank sync through an aggregator (Plaid, Setu AA, GoCardless), re-assess. The usual route is to use a licensed aggregator's AIS licence as its agent or client, not to get your own.
- General consumer-protection law (FTC Act §5 in the US; the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive and GDPR) still covers marketing claims and privacy statements, for example claims such as "your data never leaves your phone".

### Gaps
- The eCFR §1033 definitions ("covered person", "third party") were not quoted from the primary source (fetch was blocked).
- The EU PSD3/PSR reform status in 2026 was not checked. It is not expected to reach manual-entry apps, but this is not verified.

## B3. Apple App Review items for financial apps (3.2.1, 5.1.1) and whether they apply to an individual's budgeting tool

### Takeaway
Two Apple rules need an organisation or a licensed institution. 3.2.1(viii) covers "financial trading, investing, or money management" apps, which must come from the financial institution performing the service. 5.1.1(ix) covers services in "highly regulated fields such as banking and financial services", which must be submitted by a legal entity. A personal, offline expense log provides no financial service and manages no money. So on a plain reading neither rule applies, but "money management" is not defined, and that leaves some reviewer discretion.

### Cited Findings
- 3.2.1(viii): "Apps used for financial trading, investing, or money management should be submitted by the financial institution performing such services and must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where you make them available." — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.1(ix): "Apps that provide services in highly regulated fields (such as banking and financial services, healthcare, gambling, legal cannabis use, air travel and crypto exchanges) or that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer." — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Other 3.2 finance rules that do not apply: insurance apps (3.2.1(v)), binary options/CFD/FX (3.2.2(viii)), and personal-loan APR disclosure with a 36% cap (3.2.2(ix)) — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- "Money management" in 3.2.1(viii) sits beside "trading" and "investing" and names "the financial institution performing such services". Read in context, it means managing the user's money for them (robo-advisers, wealth management), not a record the user keeps. The ambiguous words "require sensitive user information" in 5.1.1(ix) are the weaker point. Spending amounts typed on the device and never sent anywhere are hard to call collected sensitive information.
- To lower review risk: in the App Review notes and the description, state that the app does not connect to banks, move money, give advice, or send data off the device. Use the Finance primary category. Make the privacy label "Data Not Collected" true.

### Gaps
- I found no official Apple statement or verified case of an individual-submitted manual budgeting app being accepted or rejected under 3.2.1(viii) or 5.1.1(ix). The many indie budgeting apps suggest acceptance, but I did not verify any individual seller example in this session. Manual check: look at the "Seller" field of a few indie Finance-category budgeting apps.

## B4. Is a "not financial advice" disclaimer recommended, and where should it go?

### Takeaway
No law requires one for a manual tracker. It is common, low-cost practice, and it is the right hedge against the SEBI "financial planning" limb (B1) and against users relying on recaps or budget suggestions. Put it in the Terms/EULA and the privacy/support page, repeat it in one line in the in-app About/Settings screen, and optionally in the App Store description.

### Cited Findings
- Example: Budget G.P.A.'s Terms say it "is a budgeting tool, not a financial advisor". Its scores, alerts and suggestions are "for informational purposes only" — [Budget G.P.A. Terms](https://budgetgpa.com/terms.html)
- "Financial advice" and "no advice" clauses are typically used to state that information is not professional advice, to tell users to get independent advice, and to limit liability — [Law Insider – Financial Advice clauses](https://lawinsider.com/clause/financial-advice); [Law Insider – No Advice clauses](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/no-advice)
- SEBI's IA registration duty covers anyone who acts as, or "hold[s] itself out as", an investment adviser — [SEBI FAQ](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/1424862077270.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested text (not reviewed by a lawyer): "TapLog is a personal record-keeping tool. It does not provide financial, investment, tax or legal advice, and it is not connected to any bank. Budgets and recaps are based only on amounts you enter."
- Placement: (1) the Terms of Use / EULA (Apple's standard EULA does not cover this, so add a short custom one or a Terms page); (2) the support/privacy page URL already in the submission kit; (3) In-app Settings → About. A disclaimer does not help if the feature itself gives investment recommendations, so feature design matters more than wording.

### Gaps
- No Indian law-firm guidance specific to disclaimers for budgeting apps was found. Legal review of the final wording is optional but advisable if paid features include any "suggestions".
