# Privacy-law and Apple privacy obligations for TapLog (offline, zero-collection iOS app) — as of 18 September 2026

Scope reminder: TapLog is an offline iOS expense tracker sold worldwide by an individual developer resident in India. All entries stay in SwiftData inside an App Group (app + widget + share extension). No network requests, no account, no analytics, no third-party SDKs, no ads. StoreKit 2 for one non-consumable, local notifications, Siri App Shortcuts (App Intents). PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy declares UserDefaults reasons 1C8F.1 and CA92.1, no collected data types, no tracking.

Legend: "LAWYER" = a point a qualified lawyer should confirm. "Secondary" = law-firm or commercial summary, not a primary text.

## Q1. India — DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025: commencement, "Data Fiduciary" status, notice and grievance duties

### Takeaway
The Rules were published in the Gazette on 13 Nov 2025 (announced 14 Nov); the core Data Fiduciary duties (notice, security, breach, rights, contact person, grievance timelines) start 18 months later, i.e. about 13 May 2027, and no amendment shortening that period has been notified. A developer whose app never lets personal data leave the user's device very probably does not "process" that data, so is not a Data Fiduciary for it; the user's own use is exempt as personal/domestic processing. The developer becomes a Data Fiduciary for any personal data it does receive (for example, support emails), and from May 2027 must then publish a contact person and a grievance route (LAWYER).

### Cited Findings
- Commencement (primary text, Rule 1): "Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 shall come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette. (3) Rule 4 shall come into force one year after the date of publication … (4) Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 shall come into force eighteen months after the date of publication" — [DPDP Rules 2025, Gazette G.S.R. 846(E)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- The Gazette issue is dated Thursday 13 November 2025 (No. 760, CG-DL-E-14112025-267650) — [DPDP Rules 2025 Gazette PDF](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf); the PIB explainer says the Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 — [PIB explainer, 17 Nov 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf). (Date discrepancy is between the Gazette date and the announcement date; computed milestones differ by one day.)
- Computed dates: Rule 4 (Consent Managers) on 13 Nov 2026; Rules 3, 5–16, 22, 23 (main obligations) on 13 May 2027; described by the site itself as "interpretation until officially confirmed" — [dpdprules.org timeline, updated 6 Sep 2026 (secondary)](https://dpdprules.org/timeline); same dates in [Mondaq (secondary)](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1830402/dpdp-act-and-rules-2025-the-2026-compliance-milestones-businesses-cant-afford-to-miss)
- In January 2026 MeitY proposed cutting the compliance window (mainly for Significant Data Fiduciaries) from 18 to 12 months — [Mondaq (secondary)](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1773554/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs); as of 6 Sep 2026 "No amendment has been officially notified" and the 18-month timeline remains operative — [dpdprules.org (secondary)](https://dpdprules.org/timeline)
- Rules 1–2 and 17–21 (in force since Nov 2025) cover definitions and the Data Protection Board; Rule 4 = Consent Managers; Rules 3, 5–16 = notice, security, breach, retention, contact person, children, rights — [Rules text](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf); phase summary also in [bitraser (secondary)](https://www.bitraser.com/article/dpdp-rules-2025.php)
- Definition: "'Data Fiduciary' means any person who alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing of personal data" (s.2(i)); "'personal data' means any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data" (s.2(t)) — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- Territorial scope: applies to processing of digital personal data in India, and outside India "if such processing is in connection with any activity related to offering of goods or services to Data Principals within the territory of India" (s.3(a)–(b)) — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- Exemption: the Act does not apply to "personal data processed by an individual for any personal or domestic purpose" (s.3(c)(i)) — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- Contact-person duty: "A Data Fiduciary shall publish … the business contact information of a Data Protection Officer, if applicable, or a person who is able to answer on behalf of the Data Fiduciary, the questions, if any, raised by the Data Principal about the processing of her personal data" (s.8(9)); "A Data Fiduciary shall establish an effective mechanism to redress the grievances of Data Principals" (s.8(10)) — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- Rule 9: every Data Fiduciary "shall prominently publish on its website or app, and mention in every response to a communication for the exercise of the rights of a Data Principal … the business contact information of the Data Protection Officer, if applicable, or a person who is able to answer on behalf of the Data Fiduciary" — [DPDP Rules 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- Rule 14(1): Data Fiduciaries must prominently publish on website or app "the details of the means using which a Data Principal may make a request for the exercise of such rights" and any identifier needed; Rule 14(3): publish the grievance response period, "not exceeding ninety days" — [DPDP Rules 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf); PIB: fiduciaries must address rights requests "within a maximum of ninety days" — [PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- Startups: s.17(3) lets the Central Government notify classes of Data Fiduciaries, "including startups", exempt from some provisions — [DPDP Act 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf). No such notification for startups was found in this research.
- Penalties: up to ₹250 crore for failing reasonable security safeguards, up to ₹200 crore for breach-notice and children's-data failures, up to ₹50 crore for other violations — [PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- Secondary summary: an organisation is a data fiduciary if it "collects personal data … for a specific purpose and determines the manner in which such personal data should be processed digitally" — [search summary citing EY / Cyril Amarchand FAQ (secondary)](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FAQs-DPDPA.pdf)

### Inferences
- The developer designs the software but never receives, stores, or accesses entries; the user decides what to record and why. The strongest reading is that the user alone processes the data for a personal/domestic purpose (s.3(c)(i) exempt), and the developer is not a Data Fiduciary for on-device entries (LAWYER: no Indian case law or Board guidance was found on on-device-only apps).
- The developer IS a Data Fiduciary for personal data it actually receives: support emails (name, email address, message content), and any data a user pastes into an email (for example, a CSV export). The Act's s.8(9)/(10) and Rules 9 and 14 apply to that processing from about 13 May 2027. Practical duty: name a contact person and an email for privacy questions and grievances in the policy and in the app, and state a response period of 90 days or less.
- A notice under s.5 / Rule 3 is needed only when consent is sought to process personal data; TapLog asks for no consent for on-device data. For support emails, the legitimate-uses basis in s.7(a) (data voluntarily provided for a specified purpose) is the likely ground, which needs no separate consent notice (LAWYER).
- Nothing in DPDP requires an Indian individual developer to appoint a DPO unless notified as a Significant Data Fiduciary; TapLog will not be one.

### Gaps
- No official MeitY or Data Protection Board guidance was found on whether an app developer is a Data Fiduciary for data that stays on the device.
- The s.7 "legitimate uses" text was not re-read in this session; confirm it covers voluntary support emails.
- I did not verify whether any startup/MSME exemption notification under s.17(3) was issued between Nov 2025 and Sep 2026 beyond the secondary report that none amended the timeline.

## Q2. GDPR — controller status for on-device processing, household exemption, Article 27 representative, and required notice content

### Takeaway
GDPR likely applies only to what the developer itself processes: support emails, and nothing on the device. Two risks need a lawyer's view. First, the household exemption covers the user but explicitly not "controllers or processors which provide the means". Second, the EDPB says a lack of access does not by itself exclude joint control. An Article 27 EU representative is very probably not needed, because the only processing (occasional support email) fits the Art 27(2)(a) exemption. The policy should still carry the Art 13 items for support email: developer identity and contact, purpose, legal basis, retention, rights, and the right to complain.

### Cited Findings
- Household exemption: GDPR does not apply to processing "by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity" (Art 2(2)(c)) — [gdpr-info.eu Art 2](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-2-gdpr/); Recital 18: the Regulation "should apply to controllers or processors which provide the means for processing personal data for such personal or household activities" — [gdpr-info.eu Recital 18](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-18/)
- EDPB: a party that "has a determinative influence on the purpose and (essential) means of the processing … is to be regarded as controller even though he or she will never have actual access to the data" (Market-research example) — [EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 on controller and processor, v2.0](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)
- EDPB para 56: "The fact that one of the parties does not have access to personal data processed is not sufficient to exclude joint controllership" (citing CJEU Jehovah's Witnesses, C-25/17) — [EDPB Guidelines 07/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)
- EDPB fn 29: "The provider of the system can be a joint controller if the criteria mentioned above are met, i.e. if the provider participates in the determination of purposes and means. Otherwise, the provider should be considered as a processor." — [EDPB Guidelines 07/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)
- Academic analysis of the same tension (device makers/software providers vs household users) — [Oxford IDPL, "Who is responsible for data processing in smart homes?" (secondary)](https://academic.oup.com/idpl/article/10/4/279/5900395)
- Territorial scope: GDPR applies to a non-EU controller processing personal data of people in the EU where the processing relates to "the offering of goods or services" to them (Art 3(2)(a)) — [gdpr-info.eu Art 3](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/)
- Art 27(1): "Where Article 3(2) applies, the controller or the processor shall designate in writing a representative in the Union." Art 27(2)(a) exempts "processing which is occasional, does not include, on a large scale, processing of special categories of data … and is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons" — [gdpr-info.eu Art 27](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-27-gdpr/)
- Art 13(1)–(2) information when data is collected from the data subject: controller identity and contact details; DPO contact if any; purposes and legal basis; legitimate interests if relied on; recipients; third-country transfers; retention period; rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, portability; right to withdraw consent; right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority; whether provision is required; automated decision-making — [gdpr-info.eu Art 13](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/)
- ePrivacy Art 5(3) (storing/accessing information on terminal equipment): EDPB says "Whenever an entity takes steps towards gaining access to information stored in the terminal equipment, Article 5(3) ePD would apply", typically where the accessing entity makes the device "send back" information — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 on technical scope of Art 5(3) ePrivacy, v2](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- On-device entries: The developer fixes the "means" (the app's code and schema) but sets no purpose of its own and gets nothing back. Under EDPB fn 29, a tool provider that does not set purposes is at most a processor, and a processor needs a controller. The user is the controller, but exempt under Art 2(2)(c). The best view is that the developer is neither controller nor processor for on-device entries. Recital 18 bites only on someone who is already a controller or processor. The EDPB "no access needed" line is the counter-argument; it arose in cases where the non-accessing party pursued its own purpose (market research, fan-page stats, preaching), which TapLog does not (LAWYER).
- Support email: the developer is clearly a controller for emails from EU users, and Art 13 applies. Likely legal basis: legitimate interests or steps at the data subject's request (Art 6(1)(b)/(f)) (LAWYER).
- Art 27: support email is "occasional", not special-category, and low risk, so the Art 27(2)(a) exemption very probably applies. No EU representative is needed (LAWYER; supervisory authorities read "occasional" narrowly).
- ePrivacy 5(3): App-local SwiftData/UserDefaults storage is done to deliver the service the user asked for, and nothing is read back to the developer. If Art 5(3) applied at all, it would fall under the "strictly necessary" exemption, so no consent prompt is needed (LAWYER; the exemption wording was not re-fetched in this session).
- Expense notes could in theory reveal special-category data (for example, health spending). Because the developer never receives them, this does not change the analysis.
- The UK GDPR has the same Art 2, 3, 13 and 27 structure; the same conclusions very probably apply (not separately researched).

### Gaps
- No EDPB or DPA guidance found that addresses a purely offline, on-device consumer app directly.
- Art 5(3) ePrivacy "strictly necessary" exemption wording and national implementations were not fetched.
- UK GDPR / UK representative requirement not separately verified.

## Q3. United States — CCPA/CPRA thresholds, CalOPPA, other state laws

### Takeaway
CCPA/CPRA does not apply: TapLog's developer is far below every threshold, and "business" status also needs collection of personal information. CalOPPA applies only to an "operator" that collects PII online, so a zero-collection app is arguably outside it. Apple requires a policy anyway, so following CalOPPA's content list (effective date, change process, Do-Not-Track statement, third-party tracking statement) costs nothing. The new state App Store Accountability Acts (Texas in force; Utah and Louisiana later) put age-signal duties on all developers regardless of data collection.

### Cited Findings
- CCPA applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California and meet one threshold: gross annual revenue over the threshold, buy/sell/share PI of 100,000+ California residents or households, or 50%+ of revenue from selling/sharing PI — [CA AG CCPA page, updated 28 Aug 2026](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa)
- The revenue threshold is CPI-adjusted every odd year; since 1 Jan 2025 it is $26,625,000 (the AG page still shows the statutory $25 million) — [CPPA, Updated Monetary Thresholds](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/cpi_adjustment.html); [privacy.ca.gov monetary thresholds](https://privacy.ca.gov/laws-and-regulations/monetary-thresholds-in-the-ccpa/)
- The "business" definition covers entities that "collect, or cause others to collect on their behalf, consumers' personal information" and meet a threshold — [Clym summary of Civ. Code 1798.140(d) (secondary)](https://www.clym.io/blog/ccpa-applicability-guide)
- CalOPPA 22575(a): "An operator of a commercial Web site or online service that collects personally identifiable information through the Internet about individual consumers residing in California … shall conspicuously post its privacy policy … or in the case of an operator of an online service, make that policy available in accordance with paragraph (5) of subdivision (b) of Section 22577." — [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 22575](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22575)
- CalOPPA required content (22575(b)): categories of PII collected and third parties shared with; the review/change process if one exists; how users are notified of material changes; effective date; how the operator responds to Do-Not-Track signals; whether other parties may collect PII across sites/services; an optional link to a DNT protocol description — [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 22575](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22575)
- CalOPPA definitions: PII means information "collected online by the operator from that individual and maintained by the operator in an accessible form"; "operator" means a person or entity that owns a website or online service "that collects and maintains personally identifiable information from a consumer residing in California"; for online services, "conspicuously post" includes "any other reasonably accessible means of making the privacy policy available" — [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 22577](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22577)
- App Store Accountability Acts: Utah, Texas and Louisiana enacted ASAAs in 2025 that put age-assurance and parental-consent obligations on app stores and developers — [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/)
- Developer duties summarized: receive age-category data (<13, 13–15, 16–17, 18+) from app stores, receive/verify parental consent data for minors, assign age ratings, and use age/consent data only for compliance and delete it after use; Texas effective 1 Jan 2026 and currently operative; Utah compliance deadline moved to 6 May 2027 with private enforcement only; Louisiana delayed to 1 Jul 2027 — [Wiley, 8 Jun 2026 (secondary)](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Key-Developments-With-State-App-Store-Accountability-Acts-as-Texas-Act-Takes-Effect)
- Texas litigation: preliminary injunction in Dec 2025; Fifth Circuit stayed it on 28 May 2026 so the law is in effect; Supreme Court denied an application to vacate the stay on 6 Jul 2026 — [Morrison Foerster (secondary)](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251111-texas-targets-app-stores-with-new-accountability-law) and search-result summaries; CONFLICT: one search summary said Louisiana's HB 570 went live 1 Jul 2026, while [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Key-Developments-With-State-App-Store-Accountability-Acts-as-Texas-Act-Takes-Effect) reports an amended date of 1 Jul 2027. The sources also disagree on whether Louisiana has a developer safe harbor ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/) says no, Wiley says reliance is allowed).

### Inferences
- CCPA: TapLog's developer is a sole proprietor far below $26.6M revenue, does not buy/sell/share PI, and does not collect PI through the app. CCPA does not apply.
- CalOPPA: Because nothing is "collected online … and maintained by the operator", TapLog is arguably not an "operator". A support email is arguably not collected "through" the app/online service (LAWYER). The low-cost safe course is to comply with 22575(b) anyway.
- Comprehensive state privacy laws (Virginia, Colorado, Texas TDPSA, etc.) use "processes/controls personal data of X consumers" thresholds; zero collection means they are not triggered (not individually verified in this session).
- COPPA governs operators that collect PI from children; with no collection, a children statement in the policy is enough (not separately fetched).
- ASAAs are not privacy-policy content rules, but they are a live US compliance item for every App Store developer. TapLog's duty is mainly to set an accurate age rating and handle any age signal Apple provides (Apple's Declared Age Range API). This belongs in the broader App Store checklist (LAWYER for Texas exposure).

### Gaps
- Exact current Louisiana effective date and safe-harbor wording (sources conflict).
- Apple's current developer guidance on Texas ASAA compliance (Declared Age Range API, significant-change consent) was not fetched.
- Other states' comprehensive privacy-law thresholds not individually confirmed.

## Q4. Apple requirements — Guideline 5.1.1, in-app policy link, App Privacy label, Siri/StoreKit, privacy manifest reason codes

### Takeaway
Apple requires a privacy policy URL in App Store Connect AND an easily accessible link inside the app, even for zero-collection apps. TapLog can truthfully answer "No, we do not collect data from this app": on-device-only data, Apple-handled payments, and Apple-collected Siri data are all outside "collected". The manifest reason codes are right but described backwards. Apple defines CA92.1 as "only accessible to the app itself" and 1C8F.1 as "the same App Group", so declaring both is correct if the app uses both standard and App Group UserDefaults. Each extension that calls UserDefaults also needs its own PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy.

### Cited Findings
- Guideline 5.1.1(i): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner." The policy must identify "what data, if any, the app/service collects, how it collects that data, and all uses of that data"; confirm third parties give equal protection; and "Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Guideline 5.1.1(v): "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Guideline 5.1.2: "Unless otherwise permitted by law, you may not use, transmit, or share someone's personal data without first obtaining their permission." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- App Store Connect: to declare no collection, "select 'No, we do not collect data from this app' and then click Save. You don't need to answer any further questions"; "You're required to provide a privacy policy URL for your iOS app platform" — [App Store Connect Help: Manage app privacy](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/manage-app-privacy/)
- Definition: "'Collect' refers to transmitting data off the device in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it for a period longer than what is necessary to service the transmitted request in real time." — [Apple, App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- "Data that is processed only on device is not 'collected' and does not need to be disclosed in your answers." — [Apple, App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- Payments: "If your app uses a payment service, the payment information is entered outside your app, and you as the developer never have access to the payment information, it is not collected and does not need to be disclosed." — [Apple, App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- Apple services: "If you collect data about your app from Apple frameworks or services, you should indicate what data you collect … You are not responsible for disclosing data collected by Apple." — [Apple, App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- Required-reason definitions (primary, NSPrivacyAccessedAPITypeReasons):
  - CA92.1: "Declare this reason to access user defaults to read and write information that is only accessible to the app itself." — [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/app-privacy-configuration/nsprivacyaccessedapitypes/nsprivacyaccessedapitypereasons)
  - 1C8F.1: "Declare this reason to access user defaults to read and write information that is only accessible to the apps, app extensions, and App Clips that are members of the same App Group as the app itself." It does not permit reading data written outside the App Group or by the system (except that the app is "not responsible" if the system returns global-domain values for a missing key) — [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/app-privacy-configuration/nsprivacyaccessedapitypes/nsprivacyaccessedapitypereasons)
  - C56D.1: third-party SDK wrapper reason; "This reason may only be declared by third-party SDKs." AC6B.1: MDM managed-config keys — [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/app-privacy-configuration/nsprivacyaccessedapitypes/nsprivacyaccessedapitypereasons)
- Declared reasons must "accurately reflect your use of each of these APIs" and be consistent with the app's functionality; since 1 May 2024 App Store Connect rejects apps that use required-reason APIs without declaring the reason — [Apple, Describing use of required reason API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api)
- "For each executable or dynamic library in an app that uses a required reason API, the bundle that includes the executable or dynamic library needs to include a privacy manifest file that reports the API." — [Apple, Describing use of required reason API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api)
- Manifest keys: NSPrivacyTracking (Boolean), NSPrivacyTrackingDomains (required only when tracking is true), NSPrivacyCollectedDataTypes, NSPrivacyAccessedAPITypes; the file must be named PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy and be in the target's resources for Xcode's privacy report — [Apple, Privacy manifest files](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files)
- EU DSA trader rules: Apple must verify and display trader contact information (address, phone, email) on the EU product page for traders; apps without a declared trader status are removed from the EU App Store — [App Store Connect Help: DSA trader requirements](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements/); [Apple news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=einwn76m)

### Inferences
- Label: select "No, we do not collect data from this app". StoreKit 2 transaction data (for example, transaction IDs) is verified on device and never sent to the developer, so it is not "collected"; Apple's own purchase data is Apple's to disclose.
- Siri/App Intents: Speech and intent resolution are handled by Apple. The app gets only the resolved intent parameters on device, so this is not "collected" by TapLog. Apple's collection is Apple's disclosure.
- Reason codes: The user's statement that the manifest declares "1C8F.1 and CA92.1" is correct as a pair. If the brief or code comments map 1C8F.1 to "app-only" and CA92.1 to "App Group", swap the comments, not the codes. If the app uses only App Group defaults (`UserDefaults(suiteName:)`) and never `UserDefaults.standard`, only 1C8F.1 is needed. SwiftUI `@AppStorage` defaults to `.standard`, which is CA92.1. Reason codes must match actual use, so audit calls in all three targets.
- Extensions: The widget and share extension are separate executables in separate bundles. If either calls UserDefaults (App Group suite or `@AppStorage`), that `.appex` needs its own PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy with 1C8F.1 (and CA92.1 if it uses standard defaults). Verify with Xcode's Generate Privacy Report on the archive.
- Other required-reason categories to check: file timestamp APIs (for example, `attributesOfItem`/`creationDate` when naming CSV exports), system boot time (`ProcessInfo.systemUptime`), and disk space. SwiftData itself is Apple's framework; Apple's frameworks carry their own manifests (not verified in this session).
- The in-app link is mandatory, not optional: put a "Privacy Policy" row in Settings that opens the hosted URL (or shows the same text offline).
- DSA: If the developer declares trader status (likely, since the app is sold), his name/address/phone/email will appear on EU product pages. Naming the developer in the privacy policy then adds no new disclosure.

### Gaps
- The exact label wording "Data Not Collected" on the product page was not quoted from an Apple page in this session (the App Store Connect option text was).
- Apple's own framework privacy manifests (SwiftData, StoreKit, WidgetKit) and whether the app must declare UserDefaults use made internally by Apple frameworks were not researched; Apple's docs say the reason applies to use "in code you write or included in a third-party SDK".
- No Apple statement found on whether App Review checks the in-app link placement specifically (only the guideline text).

## Q5. Do iCloud / device backups of App Group data change any answer?

### Takeaway
No. Backups go from the user's device to the user's own iCloud or computer under Apple's terms. The developer cannot access them, so they are not "collected" under Apple's definition, and they do not make the developer a controller or fiduciary. The policy should still say that backups include TapLog data, and that deleting in the app does not purge copies already in older backups.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's definition of "collect" requires transmission off device "in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it" — [Apple, App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- "You are not responsible for disclosing data collected by Apple." — [Apple, App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)
- DPDP and GDPR controller tests turn on who determines purpose and means of processing — [DPDP Act s.2(i)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf); [EDPB Guidelines 07/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- iCloud Backup is a user-enabled Apple service. Apple and the user decide purpose and means, and the developer neither receives nor can read the backup. The answers to Q1–Q4 are unchanged.
- The situation would change if TapLog adopted CloudKit or SwiftData iCloud sync. That data sits in the user's private CloudKit database, which the developer cannot read, and Apple treats it case by case. Re-check the label and the policy before shipping sync.
- Retention statement: after "Delete all entries" or uninstall, copies can remain in existing device/iCloud backups until the user deletes or overwrites them. Apple 5.1.1(i) asks for retention/deletion explanations, so say this.

### Gaps
- No Apple developer document was found that explicitly addresses whether app data inside iCloud device backups is "collected" by the developer; the conclusion rests on the general "collect" definition.
- Whether App Group containers are included in iCloud device backups by default was not verified from an Apple source in this session (widely understood to be yes unless files are excluded via `isExcludedFromBackup`).

## Q6. Checklist of what TapLog's privacy policy must say, and what the current draft is missing

### Takeaway
The draft covers the substance well. What it is missing is mostly identity and procedure: who the developer is, how to contact them about privacy, what happens to support emails, an effective date and change-notice method, a Do-Not-Track/third-party-tracking line, and the response period for requests. The largest gap is that the developer is not named. GDPR Art 13(1)(a) (for support emails) and DPDP s.8(9)/Rule 9 (from May 2027) both expect the controller/fiduciary and a contact person to be identified.

### Cited Findings
- Apple 5.1.1(i) minimum: what data (if any) is collected, how, and all uses; third-party protection statement; retention/deletion policy; how to revoke consent/request deletion; link in App Store Connect and in the app — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- GDPR Art 13: controller identity and contact details, purposes and legal basis, recipients, transfers, retention, data-subject rights, right to complain to a supervisory authority — [gdpr-info.eu Art 13](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/)
- DPDP s.8(9) and Rule 9: publish business contact information of a person who can answer privacy questions; Rule 14: publish the means of making rights requests and a grievance response period of 90 days or less — [DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf); [DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)
- CalOPPA 22575(b): categories collected and third parties; change-notification process; effective date; Do-Not-Track response; whether third parties may collect across services — [Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 22575](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=22575)

### Inferences
- Because TapLog receives no on-device data, most legal regimes attach only to support email. That section is the legally load-bearing part of the policy, and it is the part missing from the draft.
- Not naming the developer is the one omission that affects all three regimes. The EU product page will show the trader name anyway if trader status is declared.
- A statement that no data is collected must stay true in every build. Any later crash-reporting (including Apple's opt-in crash sharing surfaced in App Store Connect, which Apple collects), TipKit sync, CloudKit sync, or a feedback form requires updating both the policy and the label.
